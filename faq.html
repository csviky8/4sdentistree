01How do I know if invisible aligners can fix my specific teeth alignment issue, or would I need traditional braces?+

Aligners work well for mild to moderate crowding, gaps, and minor bite issues like overbite or crossbite, but severe misalignment or jaw discrepancies often still need traditional braces or a combined approach. The only reliable way to know is a hands-on exam and X-rays of your bite. Book a consultation so our orthodontic team can check your case before you commit either way. See the full range of services .

02What dental or bite problems does orthodontic treatment here actually address?+

Crowded or gapped teeth, overbites, underbites, crossbites, open bites, and even teeth that have shifted back after old orthodontic work can all be treated. Dr. Swapna Sreenivasagan, MDS (Orthodontics), PhD, leads orthodontic and dentofacial cases at the clinic. If you're unsure whether your specific bite issue qualifies, an assessment will confirm it and outline your realistic options.

03Is orthodontic treatment available for children as well as adults at this clinic?+

Yes, both age groups are treated, with separate pedodontic and orthodontic specialists on staff. Children often need early monitoring of jaw growth and space for permanent teeth, while adults are usually candidates for aligners or braces once all adult teeth are in. Bringing your child in early helps catch problems before they become bigger and more complex to fix.

04What happens during the first consultation for aligners or orthodontics?+

Your first visit is mainly an evaluation, not treatment: expect a discussion of your concerns, a bite and jaw examination, and usually X-rays or digital scans to see what's happening beneath the surface. From there, the orthodontist explains your options and expected timeline, so you leave with a clear plan rather than more questions.

05With so many specialists listed on the site, how is it decided which dentist actually treats me?+

Your treating dentist is decided by what you need, not by chance. A general or duty doctor typically examines you first, and if your case needs a specific specialty — orthodontics, implants, oral surgery, or root canal work — you're guided to the relevant specialist on the team. This means you're not left guessing who's actually qualified for your issue.

06Can this clinic handle more complex cases like dental implants or jaw surgery, not just routine treatment?+

Yes. The team includes dedicated oral and maxillofacial surgeons handling implants, dental and zygoma implants, and orthognathic (jaw correction) surgery, alongside prosthodontists for crowns, bridges, and implant restorations. That means more involved cases don't need an outside referral — they're managed by specialists already on staff, with routine dentistry covered under the same roof.

07What's the practical difference between getting aligners at a multi-specialty clinic versus a dedicated orthodontist?+

A dedicated orthodontist focuses purely on tooth movement, while a multi-specialty clinic like this one can also handle any related issue that comes up — a cavity needing a filling first, a gum problem, or a wisdom tooth complicating your bite — without sending you elsewhere. The trade-off is worth checking: ask who specifically oversees your aligner case day-to-day.

08How experienced is the team specifically in oral and maxillofacial surgery or implant placement?+

The clinic has surgeons with fellowship training in facial esthetics and implantology (including USA-accredited FICOI credentials), covering dental and zygoma implants and orthognathic surgery, plus separate prosthodontists managing the crown or bridge work that completes an implant. For procedures this involved, ask to see your treating surgeon's specific case experience during your consultation.

09What happens if my aligners feel uncomfortable, don't fit well, or aren't moving my teeth as expected?+

Some soreness for a day or two after each new tray is normal, but pain that lasts longer, an aligner that won't seat properly, or teeth that don't seem to be tracking as planned should be checked, not pushed through. Your dentist can adjust the aligner edges, review your bite, or amend the treatment plan. Don't skip check-ins if something feels off.

10What should I bring or know before my first appointment?+

Bring any previous dental X-rays or treatment records you have, a list of current medications, and details of any dental concerns or pain you've noticed. If it's for your child, their vaccination record can help too. Arriving a little early to fill out medical history forms keeps the visit unhurried and lets the dentist focus on your actual concerns.

11How quickly can I get an appointment for an urgent issue like a toothache or broken tooth?+

The clinic is open daily from 10 am to 9 pm, which gives more flexibility than a typical 9-to-5 practice for fitting in same-day pain or breakage. For anything urgent, call or message directly rather than waiting for a routine slot — describing the issue upfront helps the team prioritise you appropriately. Message the clinic on WhatsApp or call +91 94444 06704 for urgent issues.

12Does the clinic offer sedation or comfort options for patients who are nervous about dental work?+

Yes — the team includes a consultant medical practitioner in anesthesiology, which supports sedation for patients who find dental visits stressful or who need longer, more involved procedures. Options range from lighter relaxation to deeper sedation depending on your anxiety level and the treatment. Mention your concerns when booking so the right approach can be planned in advance.

13What kind of follow-up or maintenance is needed after finishing aligner treatment?+

Once your aligners are done, you'll move into wearing a retainer — typically close to full-time for the first few months, then nights-only long-term — because teeth naturally drift back without one. Skipping this step is one of the most common reasons people lose their results. Regular retention check-ins help confirm your retainer still fits properly.

14What aftercare is involved once a dental implant is placed?+

Expect a soft-food diet and gentle cleaning around the implant site for the first couple of weeks, plus follow-up visits to check healing and remove stitches if needed. Full integration with the jawbone takes a few months, so attending every scheduled review — not just the first one — is what actually protects the implant long-term, not extra effort at home.

15At what age should I first bring my child in for a dental or orthodontic check-up?+

Take your child for their first dental visit within six months of their first tooth appearing, or by their first birthday at the latest — this is the standard pediatric dentistry recommendation. Starting this early catches problems like early decay or crowding before they become harder to manage, and gets your child comfortable with dental visits from the start.
